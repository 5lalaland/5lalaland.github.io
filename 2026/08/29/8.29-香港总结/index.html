8.29-香港总结

发表于2026-08-29|更新于2026-10-05|AI Safety
|浏览量:

结束了香港的旅途了✈️
这个星期主要在学习一些天文知识

In the previous sections, we have discussed the importance of the lunar south pole , the engineering criteria , and several recent studies with their methods of normalizing different types of lunar data.

These methods are rigorous but also a little abstract.

So this part,we are going to move from theory to practice.

and I want to start with an interesting observation.

When our group was reviewing the essay, we found two well-known papers that reached different conclusions about the best site. One study focused on the Artemis III landing mission, selected Nobile Rim 2; the other study focused on a long-term lunar base, chose Cabeus B crater rim

So this raises the main question of our final section:

Could the best site change when the mission objective changes?

That is the idea we wanted to explore.

Part 2 — Our Demonstration

To test this idea, we built a simplified mission-driven TOPSIS demonstration and we also built a small interactive demo website. The following slides are actual screenshots from the website.

We selected five representative candidate regions and evaluated them using the same six criteria introduced earlier:

illumination, water access, terrain safety, communication, scientific value, and expansion potential.

The candidate sites and the criteria matrix remain fixed, but he weight of each criterion changes from one mission to another.
We designed four scenarios:

a Balanced Base, a Science Mission, a Resource Mission, and an Engineering Mission.

TOPSIS then recalculates the ranking for each scenario.

And It should be emphasized that this is a simple demonstration not a complete GIS raster analysis.

Furthermore ,We can also open a detail page for the winning site to inspect its major weighted factors and its representative lunar surface environment.
And as you can see in this four rankings:
the candidate sites do not change, but the ranking changes when the mission priorities change.

Besides,We also wanted to connect the numerical ranking with the actual lunar environment.

In this part, we used real lunar imagery from LROC

For the balanced scenario, Nobile Rim 2 stands out as the most well-rounded option, because its performance is evenly distributed across all criteria.

For the science scenario, Haworth benefits from its high scientific-value proxy and its complex polar geology.

For the resource scenario, we focus especially on permanently shadowed regions, or PSRs.

The Haworth image may look unusually dark, but that is actually meaningful: PSRs receive extremely little direct sunlight and may preserve water ice and other volatile materials.

Finally, when engineering operability is emphasized, Malapert Massif performs strongly in illumination, terrain safety, and communication.

So these images help us move from an abstract TOPSIS score to a physical lunar environment.

Of course, a real engineering decision would require better data, better models, and stricter constraints.

So our model is best understood as a way to explore how mission priorities affect decisions, rather than as a final recommendation for where to build a lunar base.

To conclude our whole presentation, we started with one question:

Where should we build a future research base at the lunar south pole?

After considering the scientific, resource, and engineering requirements together, our final result tend to another important point:

there may be no universally best site.

Thus rather than ask

“Where should we go?”

It is better to ask

“What do we want to do there?”

I hope our work can provide some useful insights.

Finally, as the final speaker of the final group, I would like to share some personal thoughts.
The sentence
“You’re not always going to be the strongest or the fastest, so be the smartest.”
comes from a movie called The Odyssey, which I watched last week in Hong Kong.

I think this sentence is especially meaningful in an era of rapid development of artificial intelligence.
We may not think fastest or work harder without rest than AI
but our wisdom, creativity, and courage remain uniquely human.

In other words, we should not be too optimistic about AI,
but about ourselves.

Thank you for all the leaders, teachers, and classmates who made this summer wonderful .
